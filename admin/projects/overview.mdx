A **project** is a larger piece of work that runs over several visits and documents — a modernisation, an installation, a major repair. It holds the supplier's quote, your quote to the customer, the
purchase order, the jobs, the invoice, and every document in between, in one place.

## When to use a project instead of a job

A [job](/admin/jobs/overview) is a single visit to a building. That is all it is meant to be, and for routine maintenance it is enough.

**Use a project when one job is not enough to hold the work.** The work runs over several visits, or several jobs, or is partly done by a subcontractor, and the paperwork piles up around it:

* You have to get a price from a supplier before you can quote the customer.
* The customer has to accept a quote before anything is scheduled.
* You raise a purchase order to your supplier.
* You want one bundle of paperwork to send the customer at the end.

A door operator replacement across several visits is a project. A monthly service is not — and neither is a single-visit repair you happen to quote for first, which is a job with a quote against it.

<Note>
  Projects is switched on per workspace. If you cannot see **Projects** in the
  sidebar, an owner can turn it on under **Account → Features → Projects**. Only
  owners and admins can see projects at all — technicians never do.
</Note>

## The mental model: six stages

Every project has the same six stages, in the same order, for every customer. They cannot be added to, renamed or reordered.

| Stage | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Supplier quote** | What your supplier is charging you. Internal. |
| **Customer quote** | What you are charging the customer. |
| **Procurement** | The order you place with your supplier. Internal. |
| **Work** | The jobs and their records. |
| **Invoice** | The bill to the customer. |
| **Closed** | The project is finished and frozen. |

Two of them — **Supplier quote** and **Procurement** — are marked **Internal** in
the app. Nothing on an internal stage is ever sent to the building manager.

<Note>
  The stage the app calls **Procurement** is the purchase order you place with your supplier. The [glossary](/start/glossary) lists it under **Purchase order**; they are the same stage.
</Note>

LiftAuth works out how far a project has got by itself, from the documents on it.
You do not drag a card between columns: accept a quote, sign a job, issue an
invoice, and the project moves. Where you *do* have to say so — a supplier quote
that only exists as a PDF, for example — the stage has a **Mark complete** button.
