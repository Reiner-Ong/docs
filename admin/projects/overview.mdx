A **project** is a folder for one piece of customer work that involves more than a
single visit — it holds the supplier's quote, your quote to the customer, the
purchase order, the jobs, the invoice, and every document in between, in one place.

## When to use a project instead of a job

A [job](/admin/jobs) is one technician visit to one lift. That is all it is meant to
be, and for routine maintenance it is enough.

Use a project when the work is a small piece of commerce rather than a visit:

* You have to get a price from a supplier before you can quote the customer.
* The customer has to accept a quote before anything is scheduled.
* You raise a purchase order to your supplier.
* The work takes several jobs, or is partly done by a subcontractor.
* You want one bundle of paperwork to send the customer at the end.

A door operator replacement is a project. A monthly service is not.

<Note>
  Projects is switched on per workspace. If you cannot see **Projects** in the
  sidebar, an owner can turn it on under **Account → Features → Projects**. Only
  owners and admins can see projects at all — technicians never do.
</Note>

## The mental model: six stages

Every project has the same six stages, in the same order, for every customer. You
cannot add, remove or rename them.

| Stage | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Supplier quote** | What your supplier is charging you. Internal. |
| **Customer quote** | What you are charging the customer. |
| **Procurement** | The order you place with your supplier. Internal. |
| **Work** | The jobs and the service records. |
| **Invoice** | The bill to the customer. |
| **Closed** | The project is finished and frozen. |

Two of them — **Supplier quote** and **Procurement** — are marked **Internal** in
the app. Nothing on an internal stage is ever sent to the building manager.

LiftAuth works out how far a project has got by itself, from the documents on it.
You do not drag a card between columns: accept a quote, sign a job, issue an
invoice, and the project moves. Where you *do* have to say so — a supplier quote
that only exists as a PDF, for example — the stage has a **Mark complete** button.

The board column a project sits in is the **leftmost stage that is not yet done or
skipped**. A project can be filled in out of order; the column tells you the
earliest thing still outstanding.

## Finding your projects

Click **Projects** in the sidebar. You get two readings of the same set of
projects:

* **Board** — six columns, one per stage, with a card per project. This is the default.
* **List** — one table across all six stages, with customer, building, stage,
  assignees and last update. Typing in the search box moves you here, because a
  search is a question the six columns cannot answer.

Filter by customer, by person, or with **Assigned to me**. The **Show closed** chip
hides closed projects. Filters and the chosen view live in the page address, so a
link you paste to a colleague opens exactly what you were looking at.

<Tip>
  A card can carry one badge. **Ready** means this stage's turn has come and it is
  waiting on somebody — the same prompt appears in your [Inbox](/admin/inbox). A
  review badge outranks it, because the review is what the project is waiting on.
</Tip>
