title: "How to find a record in Drive"
description: "Open the right building folder and narrow the list down to the visit you are looking for."

---

Every service <Tooltip tip="The report produced when a technician fills in a form and submits it — the item results, photos, notes and signatures." cta="Glossary" href="/start/glossary#work-on-site">record</Tooltip> is filed in the folder of the building it was carried out in. Finding one is two steps: open the building, then narrow the list.

## Open the building folder

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to Drive">
    Click **Drive** in the left sidebar, under **Operations**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Find the building">
    Type part of the building's name into the **Search buildings or customers…**
    box. Folders that do not match disappear as you type.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Open it">
    Click the folder. The subtitle under each folder name tells you what is inside
    — either how many lifts your firm has in that building, or, for a building you
    hold records for but own no lift in, how many records the folder holds.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Records for a lift that is not attached to any building are in the folder called
  **Unassigned**, at the bottom of the Buildings list.
</Tip>

## Narrow the list down

Inside a building folder each row is one record — that is, one form a technician submitted, so a visit that covered several forms shows up as several rows. There are three ways to cut the
list down, and they work together.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Search by reference or lift" icon="magnifying-glass">
    The **Search records…** box matches the record's reference and the [BCA Equipment ID](/start/glossary#work-on-site) of the lifts the visit covered — but it only filters **the rows on the page you are currently viewing**, not the whole folder.

    That matters for what a result means. If a search for a lift's BCA Equipment ID returns nothing, it tells you nothing on _this page_ matched. It does **not** tell you the lift has no earlier visits — those visits are most likely on another page, where the search box never looked.

    To see a lift's whole history, use the two controls that do cover the whole folder: sorting and paging. Click **Date** to sort, then work through the pages, searching each one as you go. Oldest-first puts the earliest visit on record on the first page and newest-first the most recent, so either end of the history is one click away.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Sort by date" icon="arrow-down-wide-short">
    Click the **Date** column heading to switch between newest-first and
    oldest-first. The date shown is the date the visit was carried out, not the
    date the report was filed.

    Sorting applies to the whole folder, so oldest-first is the quickest way to
    reach the very first visit on record.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Page through the folder" icon="chevron-right">
    Long folders are paged. The page controls at the bottom of the table show how
    many records the folder holds in total.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Read a record

Click any row to open the record's PDF in a preview window, without leaving Drive.
The panel beside the document shows the record's sign-off status and, if the
record mentions any [issues](/admin/issues), links straight through to them.

Close the preview to return to the folder — you have not changed anything by opening it, and nothing here can change the record. **A record cannot be corrected.** It cannot be edited once it exists, by anyone: a signed record cannot even be deleted, and an unsigned one can be deleted by an owner or admin but never amended.

<Note>
  A row marked **— no document** is a **past visit** — one somebody added with **Record past visits** on a building page or a maintenance contract page, rather than a technician submitting a form. It has no job behind it, no item results and no signature, only the date, the lifts, the technician and notes. When no legacy PDF was attached there is nothing to open. See [What ends up in Drive](/admin/drive/reference#what-ends-up-in-drive).
</Note>
