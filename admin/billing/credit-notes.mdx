---
title: "How to credit, refund or write off an invoice"
description: "Reverse part or all of an invoice you have already issued, using a credit note."
icon: "rotate-left"
---

An issued invoice can never be edited or deleted — the customer holds it, and your accounts count it. A **credit note** is the one supported way to reverse it. It is a document in its own right, with its own number and PDF, and it reduces what the customer owes.

## Raise a credit note

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the invoice from the ledger">
    Click the invoice row in **Billing → Ledger**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click Raise credit note">
    The form opens with the customer and invoice already filled in.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose the kind">
    - **Adjustment (correct an over-bill)** — you billed too much.
    - **Refund (money returned)** — you are handing money back. You rarely pick this by hand: refunding a payment raises a refund credit note for you automatically.
    - **Write-off (bad debt)** — you have given up on collecting it.

    The kind is a label for your own records. All three reduce the balance the same way.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Enter the amount and currency">
    Credit part of the invoice or all of it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Give a reason">
    A short line explaining why — for example, "Goodwill — delayed call-out". It prints on the credit note.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Save">
    The credit note lands on the ledger as a negative amount and the invoice's balance drops immediately.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  You can also start from **+ New billing → Credit note** — "Reduce what a customer owes". Do this when the credit is not tied to one particular invoice.
</Tip>

## Credit against an invoice, or against the account

| You raised it | Status it starts in | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Against an invoice** | **Applied** | The whole amount is applied to that invoice straight away, capped at what the invoice still owes. |
| **Against the customer, with no invoice** | **Open** | The credit sits on the account until you apply it. It shows in **Credit outstanding**. |

## Apply an open credit note

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the credit note from the ledger">
    Click the row. Open notes carry an **Open** badge.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click Apply to invoice">
    Choose which of the customer's open invoices to draw the credit down against, and how much.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Save">
    The invoice balance drops. Once the whole credit note has been drawn down it becomes **Applied**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The **Apply to invoice** action disappears once nothing is left to apply.

## Cancelling a credit note

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the credit note and click Void credit note">
    Confirm.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Voiding cancels the credit the note carries. Any invoice it was applied to goes back up by that amount. Voiding is one-way.

<Note>
  A voided credit note stays visible on the ledger with a **Void** badge, and its PDF is still downloadable. It is a document the customer may be holding, so LiftAuth keeps it on record — it simply stops moving money.
</Note>

## Automatic refund credit notes

When a card or PayNow payment is refunded, LiftAuth raises the credit note for you — kind **Refund**, already applied, linked back to the payment it reverses. You do not need to raise one by hand, and you should not delete the original payment afterwards: the credit note points at it.

## Credit note statuses

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Open** | Credit the customer holds, not yet applied to any invoice |
| **Applied** | Fully drawn down against one or more invoices |
| **Void** | Cancelled. Credits nothing. |

<Note>
  Credit notes are owner and admin only, including simply reading the list.
</Note>
