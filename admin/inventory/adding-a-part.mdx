A **part** is one kind of spare component in your catalogue — "Door Operator Motor", not the four of them sitting on a shelf. You create it once, and from then on you record stock against it.

<Note>
  Add your [warehouses](/admin/inventory/setting-up-warehouses) before your parts. The **Initial warehouse** field below is always on the dialog, but it is disabled until at least one warehouse exists — its placeholder reads _No warehouses yet_ — and without it the new part starts at zero on hand.
</Note>

## Create the part

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to Inventory">
    Click **Inventory** in the left sidebar, then open the **Parts** tab.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click + Add Part">
    The **Add Part** dialog opens.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Enter the name">
    A plain description of the component, for example *Door Operator Motor*. This is what your team will search for later, so use the words they actually say.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose a Family and a Brand">
    **Family** is the category — Doors, Safety gear, Electrical & control, and so on. **Brand** is the manufacturer, or **Generic** if it fits anything. Both are required, and both become filters on the Parts list. The full lists are in the [reference](/admin/inventory/reference#part-families).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Enter the prices (optional)">
    **Cost price** is what the part costs you to buy. **Unit price** is your catalogue list price — the figure that fills in automatically when you add this part to a quote or an invoice. A price you leave out is stored as **0**, not as blank, so an unpriced part reads as `0` afterwards. You can set a real figure later.

    <Note>
      Parts and [standard billing items](/admin/billing/standard-items) do not work the same way here. A standard billing item really can have no price at all; a part always has a number, and for an unpriced part that number is `0`.
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Enter the part code (optional)">
    The manufacturer's code printed on the part itself, for example *DOA-200*. Up to 64 characters. This is not the same as the Part No. that LiftAuth assigns — see [Two numbers](#two-numbers-and-why) below.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the opening stock (optional)">
    Pick an **Initial warehouse**, then type the **Initial quantity** you already hold there. Both boxes are always shown; the warehouse picker is disabled until a warehouse exists, and the quantity box stays disabled until you have chosen one. LiftAuth records this as the part's first stock movement.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add at least one photo">
    Photos are required — you cannot save without one. Add up to 5 images, JPEG, PNG or WebP, each up to 10 MB.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click Add Part">
    The part is created and appears at the top of the Parts list.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  The photo is the fastest way for a technician to confirm they have the right component in their hand on site, so take it in good light and get the whole part in frame.
</Tip>

<h2 id="two-numbers-and-why">
  Two numbers, and why
</h2>

Each part carries two identifiers, and they are not interchangeable.

| Field | Who sets it | Looks like | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Part No.** | LiftAuth, automatically | `PRT-2026-A1B2C3` | LiftAuth's own reference for the part. You cannot edit it. Use it when talking to colleagues about a catalogue entry. |
| **Part code** | You, optionally | `DOA-200` | The code the manufacturer printed on the component. Use it when ordering from a supplier. |

The search box on the Parts list matches the **name** and the **Part No.** — it does not match the part code, so search for a part by its name.

There is nowhere to keep your firm's own existing internal part numbers: there is no dedicated field for them and no notes or custom-field column on a part. If you are coming from another system, its numbers cannot be carried over — plan on the LiftAuth Part No. becoming the reference your team uses.

## Changing a part later

Open the part from the Parts list and click **Edit details**. You can change the **name**, **family**, **brand**, **cost price**, and **unit price**.

The **Part No.** cannot be changed, and stock levels are not edited here — those only ever change through a [stock movement](/admin/inventory/moving-stock).

## Archiving a part you no longer stock

Archiving hides a part from the everyday list without deleting its history. Nothing is lost — the movements it accumulated stay on record.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the part">
    From **Inventory → Parts**, click the part.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click Archive">
    Confirm in the **Archive part?** dialog by clicking **Archive part**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

An archived part shows an **Archived** badge, disappears from the default Parts list, and loses its **Move stock** button, so its counts stop changing. To see archived parts again, click **Show archived** on the Parts list. To bring one back, open it and click **Unarchive** — that takes effect immediately, with no confirmation step.

<Warning>
  Parts cannot be deleted, only archived. If a part was created by mistake, archive it.
</Warning>
