This page follows one part from your catalogue onto a repair job and through to the bill.

## How a part gets onto a job

A part reaches a job through a quote.

<Steps>
  <Step title="You quote the work">
    When you build a quote, you can add a **Part** line. Pick the part from your catalogue and LiftAuth fills in the description and the **unit price** you set on it. Change the quantity and price on the line if this job is different.
  </Step>

  <Step title="The quote is accepted and converted">
    Converting an accepted quote creates the repair <Tooltip tip="A visit to fix a specific, previously reported fault." cta="Glossary" href="/start/glossary">job</Tooltip>.
  </Step>

  <Step title="The job carries a Parts required list">
    Every part line on the quote becomes a row in a **Parts required** section on the job, showing the description, **Qty**, and a **Pending** badge. Click a row to open that part in Inventory.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The wording and quantity on the job are a snapshot of the quote at the moment it was converted. Renaming the part in the catalogue afterwards does not change what the job says it needs — the job keeps the wording the customer was quoted on.

## Taking the parts out of stock

<Warning>
  LiftAuth does **not** deduct stock automatically. A part sitting on a job's **Parts required** list stays **Pending** for the life of the job. Nothing comes off your shelf count until somebody records a withdrawal.
</Warning>

So the physical step and the bookkeeping step are separate, and both need doing:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the job and read the Parts required list">
    Note the part and the quantity.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Take the part off the shelf">
    Or hand it to the technician, or load it into the van.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Record a Withdraw movement">
    Open the part — clicking its row on the job takes you straight there — click **Move stock**, choose **Withdraw (remove stock)**, pick the warehouse it came out of, enter the quantity, and put the job's reference in the note. Full steps are in [Moving stock](/admin/inventory/moving-stock).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Always name the job in the movement note, for example *REP-2026-4F1A — door motor*. That note is the only link between the withdrawal and the visit it was for.
</Tip>

If the technician takes the part from a van, record the withdrawal against **that van**, not the main store. If it was moved to the van earlier in the week, that earlier move should have been recorded as a [transfer](/admin/inventory/moving-stock#transfer-move-stock-between-warehouses).

## Getting the part onto the bill

The quote's part lines are what the customer is charged for. When the job completes and the invoice is drafted from the quote, those lines carry across — you do not re-enter them.

For work that was **not** quoted — a part fitted on the day — add the part as a line on the invoice itself. Open the invoice, add a **Part** line, and pick the part; the description and unit price come from the catalogue. Then record the withdrawal in Inventory as above. See [Invoices & Payments](/admin/invoices-and-payments).

<Note>
  Billing and stock are two separate records. Adding a part line to an invoice does not change your stock, and recording a withdrawal does not bill anybody. Do both.
</Note>

## Quick check before you close a job

* Is every part on the **Parts required** list actually fitted?
* Has a **Withdraw** movement been recorded for each one, from the right warehouse?
* Do the quantities on the invoice match what was fitted?
