A **record template** is the form a technician fills in on their phone during a
job. It decides what they are asked, in what order, and what ends up on the
[record](/admin/records) the building manager signs.

You find them under **Record Templates** in the left sidebar, in the
**Operations** group.

***

## How a template becomes a record

<Steps>
  <Step title="You build the template">
    You add sections and [items](/start/glossary#work-on-site) — things like _Machine room clean_, _Door operation_, _Rope tension reading_. Each item has a type that decides how the technician answers it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="A job uses the template">
    LiftAuth never picks a template for you — a person always names it, but not always on the job itself.

    - On **recurring maintenance**, someone names the template once per [coverage group](/start/glossary#work-on-site) in the maintenance contract builder. Each visit the contract generates inherits that choice as a snapshot stamped when the job is generated, so editing the contract afterwards does not re-point jobs that already exist.
    - On **any other job**, whoever creates or schedules it names the template on each form the job carries.

    A form with no template named for it is a notes-only form — free text and photos, with no items.
  </Step>

  <Step title="The technician fills it in">
    In the mobile app the technician works through your items, adds remarks and
    photos where needed, and signs.
  </Step>

  <Step title="LiftAuth produces the record">
    Each submitted form becomes its own [record](/admin/records) — a job carrying three forms produces three records — which the building manager signs and which can be downloaded as a PDF.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A template is a *mould*, not a copy. Every form on a job stamps out a fresh record from
it, and each record keeps its own answers forever — even if you later change the
template.

## How many templates you need

<CardGroup cols={1}>
  <Card title="Maintenance — as many as you like" icon="calendar-check">
    Different lifts need different checks. Have one template for traction lifts,
    another for hydraulics, another for escalators. You choose which one applies
    to which lifts when you set up the building's maintenance contract.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Repair — one, in practice" icon="badge-alert">
    The **New record template** dialog stops offering **Repair** once you have an active repair template, so a workspace normally has exactly one. It is still chosen by hand: whoever creates a repair job picks the template for each form on it.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Breakdown — one, in practice" icon="badge-x">
    The dialog stops offering **Breakdown** the same way. Again, nothing selects it for you — the person raising the call-out picks it on the job.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The one-per-type limit lives in the **New record template** dialog only, and only for Repair and Breakdown. Nothing underneath enforces it, so a restore can leave you with two active templates of the same type. See [How to archive or restore a record template](/admin/record-templates/archiving-a-record-template).

When your workspace was created, LiftAuth gave you a starter set — a Singapore maintenance template plus a blank repair and a blank breakdown template. They are yours: edit them freely.

***

## Getting started quickly

If you have never opened this page before, start from the maintenance template your workspace was given: open **Record Templates**, click into it, and rename and adjust it to match how you actually service a lift.

[How to create a record template](/admin/record-templates/creating-a-record-template) walks through every field, including the job type — which **cannot be changed once the template is created** — and the estimated time per form, which is what LiftAuth uses to work out how long a site visit should be.

<Note>
  Only **Owners** and **Admins** can create or change record templates.
  Record Templates is one of the areas hidden from a technician-only account: technicians fill the forms in from the mobile app instead.
</Note>
