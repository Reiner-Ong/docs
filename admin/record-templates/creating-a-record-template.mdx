---
title: "How to create a record template"
description: "Build the form your technicians fill in, from a blank template to a saved one."
icon: "pencil"
---

This guide takes you from an empty template to a saved one your technicians can
use. You need to be an **Owner** or an **Admin**.

***

## 1. Create the template

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Record Templates">
    Click **Record Templates** in the left sidebar, under **Operations**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click New record template">
    The button is at the top right of the page.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Give it a name">
    Type something your team will recognise — for example *High-rise traction*.
    The name must be unique among your active templates of the same job type.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose this template's job type">
    Pick **Maintenance**, **Repair** or **Breakdown**. This sets the job type of **this template**, and nothing else — it does not put a type on any job. A job has no type of its own: what makes a visit maintenance, a repair or a breakdown is the template behind each form on it, so a job whose forms mix templates has no single kind.

    You can have as many Maintenance templates as you like. The dialog stops offering **Repair** or **Breakdown** once you have an active template of that type, which is why a workspace normally has one of each. A template's job type can also be a custom type of its own, which this dialog does not offer.

    <Warning>
      The job type **cannot be changed later**. If you pick the wrong one you
      have to create a new template.
    </Warning>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the estimated time per form">
    How many minutes one technician needs to fill in one of these records.
    LiftAuth adds this up across all the lifts on a maintenance contract to work
    out how long the site visit should be, so a rough-but-honest number is
    better than a guess of 30.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click Create">
    The template opens in the editor, on the **Content** tab.
  </Step>
</Steps>

***

## 2. Add your sections and items

The **Content** tab shows the template as it will look on a real record —
letterhead at the top, then the **Form**, which is the part you edit.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Name the first section">
    Type a heading into **Section title** — for example *Machine room*. The first
    section's title is optional; leave it blank for a flat list with no heading.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add an item">
    At the bottom of the section there is a row of coloured chips. Click the one
    matching the kind of answer you want:

    | Chip | Use it for |
    | --- | --- |
    | **+ Pass/Fail/NA** | Anything you inspect and judge |
    | **+ Text** | A description or free-form note |
    | **+ Select** | A choice from a list you define |
    | **+ Number** | A measurement or reading |
    | **+ Date** | A calendar date |
    | **+ Photo** | Photos taken on site |

    Full detail on each type is in the
    [reference](/admin/record-templates/reference#item-types).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Write the item title">
    Type into **Item title** — this is exactly what the technician reads on their
    phone, so write it as an instruction: *Check brake pads for wear*, not
    *Brakes*.

    Every item must have a title. A blank one is a saving error, not an
    ignored row.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Fill in the options, for a Select item">
    A **Select** item shows an **Options (at least two)** list. Type each choice,
    and use **+ Add option** for more. Fewer than two filled options blocks
    saving.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add more sections">
    Click **Add section** at the bottom of the form for the next group of items.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Drag the handle at the left of an item row to reorder it within its section.
</Tip>

***

## 3. Mark items as required, or show their history

Each item row has a small sliders button on the right (its tooltip reads
**History & required**). Click it to turn on either modifier:

- **Required** — *Block submit until answered*. The technician cannot submit the
  record while it is blank. Use it sparingly: too many required items and
  technicians get stuck on site.
- **Show history** — *Show last value + date on the form*. The technician sees
  what was recorded for this item at the previous visit, and when. Ideal for
  readings you compare over time. Not available on Photo items.

Both modifiers appear as small tags under the item title once they are on.

***

## 4. Set the estimated time

Scroll to **Settings** below the form and check **Estimated time**. It must be
greater than zero — LiftAuth will not save the template otherwise.

***

## 5. Save

Click **Save changes** at the top right. An **Unsaved** badge sits in the header
until you do.

If something is wrong, the page tells you at the top — for example *2 fields need
attention before you can save — they're highlighted below* — and scrolls to the
first problem. Fix the highlighted fields and save again.

***

## Copying an existing template instead

If a new template is mostly the same as one you already have:

1. Open the existing template, or find it in the list.
2. Click **Duplicate**.
3. LiftAuth creates *Copy of …* with the same items and job type, and opens it.
4. Rename it and adjust.

Duplicate copies the **last saved** version, so save your edits first.

<Note>
  **Duplicate** is only offered on Maintenance templates and on templates you
  authored. Repair and Breakdown are limited to one each, and library forms
  cannot be duplicated.
</Note>

***

## What happens next

Nothing yet — a template does no work until a form on a job uses it, and LiftAuth never picks a template for you.

- A **Maintenance** template needs to be put onto lifts through a maintenance
  contract, which is what makes the recurring visits use it. See
  [How to assign a record template to lifts](/admin/record-templates/assigning-a-record-template).
- A **Repair** or **Breakdown** template is not assigned anywhere in advance. Whoever schedules the job chooses it on each form, so the template only gets used if they pick it.
