A **maintenance contract** is the servicing agreement you hold with a building. It says which lifts you service, how often, who does the work, how the building is billed, and when the agreement runs out. Once it is set up, LiftAuth plans all of the contract's **visits** — turning each one into a real [job](/admin/jobs/overview) fourteen days before it is due — and, when the contract is billed automatically, drafts the invoices on that schedule too.

You will find them under **Maintenance Contracts** in the left-hand navigation.

## Contract, or "Maintenance Plan"?

They are the same idea, renamed. Older LiftAuth guidance describes a maintenance plan attached to a single **lift**. In the dashboard today that has been replaced by a contract attached to a single **building**:

| Then | Now |
| --- | --- |
| One maintenance plan per lift | One maintenance contract per building |
| Created from the lift's detail page | Created from **Maintenance Contracts** or a building's page |
| One lift, one frequency | Many lifts, sorted into coverage groups, each with its own frequency |
| No start and end dates | A fixed contract term, with an expiry |

So if you are looking for the "Create maintenance plan" button on a lift, it is now **+ New Contract**, and you pick the building rather than the lift.

## The mental model

A contract holds four things, and each one drives something downstream:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Coverage" icon="elevator" href="/admin/maintenance-contracts/create-a-contract">
    Which lifts are serviced, sorted into [**coverage groups**](/start/glossary) — the builder labels these "Record 1", "Record 2" on screen. Each group is one set of lifts, one [record template](/start/glossary), and its own frequency (Monthly or Quarterly). A contract whose groups differ reads **Mixed**.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Schedule" icon="calendar" href="/admin/maintenance-contracts/reference#how-the-automation-is-timed">
    The assigned technician or team, the preferred day of the month, the start time, and how long a visit takes. This is what produces the **upcoming visits**.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Billing" icon="file-invoice" href="/admin/maintenance-contracts/reference#billing-cadences">
    How the building is invoiced — per visit, once a year, or not automatically at all — plus the invoice lines every generated invoice carries.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Term" icon="hourglass" href="/admin/maintenance-contracts/reference#statuses">
    The start month and the last service month. When the term ends the contract expires and stops producing work.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

From those, LiftAuth works out every visit for the whole term ahead and lists them on the contract as **upcoming visits**. Fourteen days before each one, it turns that visit into a real [job](/admin/jobs/overview) with the technician and time already filled in.

The calendar calls the same thing a [**planned job**](/start/glossary): a planned job on the calendar is exactly one row on the contract's **Upcoming Visits** tab, and it cannot be dragged there — the calendar refuses with "This job hasn't been generated yet — it'll be scheduled automatically closer to the date." Move it from the contract instead, as [How to reschedule and backfill visits](/admin/maintenance-contracts/manage-visits) describes.

<Note>
  Everything after that follows the normal job flow: the technician completes the visit, the building manager signs the [record](/admin/records), and you close the job. Nothing about a contract changes how a job is worked.
</Note>

### Where all of this starts

All four begin in the same place: **Maintenance Contracts** in the left-hand navigation. **\+ New Contract** opens the builder; clicking a row opens the contract, where **Edit**, **Archive contract** and the **Upcoming Visits** tab live. Only **Active** contracts can be edited. The pages above carry the steps.

## Before you start

A contract needs its building already set up, so make sure you have:

- [Added the lifts](/admin/lifts) and put them in the right building.
- [Added the building manager](/admin/building-managers) and assigned them to the building. If your organisation bills [building managers](/start/glossary), LiftAuth will not let you create a contract for a building that has none.
- At least one maintenance [record template](/start/glossary), so you can pick the checklist each coverage group is inspected against. Nothing picks one for you.
