A **maintenance contract** is the servicing agreement you hold with a building. It says which lifts you service, how often, who does the work, how the building is billed, and when the agreement runs out. Once it is set up, LiftAuth creates the maintenance [jobs](/admin/jobs) — the service visits your technicians attend — and the draft invoices on that schedule for you.

You will find them under **Maintenance Contracts** in the left-hand navigation.

## Contract, or "Maintenance Plan"?

They are the same idea, renamed. Older LiftAuth guidance — and the [Maintenance Plans](/admin/maintenance-plans) page — describes a plan attached to a single **lift**. In the dashboard today that has been replaced by a contract attached to a single **building**:

| Then | Now |
| --- | --- |
| One maintenance plan per lift | One maintenance contract per building |
| Created from the lift's detail page | Created from **Maintenance Contracts** or a building's page |
| One lift, one frequency | Many lifts, grouped into record entries, each with its own frequency |
| No start and end dates | A fixed contract term, with an expiry |

So if you are looking for the "Create maintenance plan" button on a lift, it is now **+ New Contract**, and you pick the building rather than the lift.

## The mental model

A contract holds four things, and each one drives something downstream:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Coverage" icon="elevator">
    Which lifts are serviced, grouped into **record entries**. Each entry is one set of lifts, one record template, and one frequency (monthly or quarterly).
  </Card>

  <Card title="Schedule" icon="calendar">
    The assigned technician or team, the preferred day of the month, the start time, and how long a visit takes. This is what produces the **upcoming visits**.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Billing" icon="file-invoice">
    How the building is invoiced — per visit, once a year, or not automatically at all — plus the invoice lines every generated invoice carries.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Term" icon="hourglass">
    The start month and the last service month. When the term ends the contract expires and stops producing work.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

From those, LiftAuth works out every visit for the whole term ahead and lists them on the contract as **upcoming visits**. Fourteen days before each one, it turns that visit into a real [job](/admin/jobs) with the technician and time already filled in.

<Note>
  Everything after that follows the normal job flow: the technician completes the visit, the building manager signs the [record](/admin/records), and you close the job. Nothing about a contract changes how a job is worked.
</Note>

### Starting each job

- **Setting one up** — open **Maintenance Contracts**, click **+ New Contract**, and choose the building. The builder opens on the coverage step. Full walkthrough: [How to set up a maintenance contract](/admin/maintenance-contracts/create-a-contract).
- **Moving a visit** — open the contract, go to the **Upcoming Visits** tab, and click **Reschedule** on the row. Full walkthrough: [How to reschedule and backfill visits](/admin/maintenance-contracts/manage-visits).
- **Changing one** — open the contract and click **Edit** in the header. Only active contracts can be edited. Full walkthrough: [How to change or end a maintenance contract](/admin/maintenance-contracts/edit-or-end-a-contract).

## Before you start

A contract needs its building already set up, so make sure you have:

- [Added the lifts](/admin/lifts) and put them in the right building.
- [Added the building manager](/admin/building-managers) and assigned them to the building. If your organisation bills building managers, LiftAuth will not let you create a contract for a building that has none.
- At least one maintenance record template, so each record entry has a checklist to resolve to.
