---
title: "How to set up a maintenance contract"
description: "Create a servicing agreement for a building so LiftAuth schedules its visits and drafts its invoices."
icon: "plus"
---

Setting up a contract is a five-step builder. At the end, LiftAuth writes out every visit for the whole contract term and starts turning them into [jobs](/admin/jobs/overview) as each one comes due.

<Note>
  Before you start, the building needs its [lifts](/admin/lifts) added and — if your organisation bills building managers — a [building manager](/admin/building-managers) assigned. Without a building manager, LiftAuth will refuse to create the contract.
</Note>

## Open the builder

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to Maintenance Contracts">
    Click **Maintenance Contracts** in the left-hand navigation.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Click + New Contract">
    A small dialog asks which building the contract covers.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose the building and click Continue">
    The builder opens on step 1. A progress bar at the top shows which step you are on.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  You can also start from the building itself: open the building, find the **Maintenance Contracts** section, and click **+ New Contract** there. It skips the building question.
</Tip>

## Step 1 — Records

This step decides **which lifts are serviced and how often**.

A contract is built from [**coverage groups**](/start/glossary) — the builder labels these "Record 1", "Record 2" on screen, but they are not the signed [record](/admin/records) a technician submits. One coverage group is a set of lifts, serviced together, against one [record template](/start/glossary), at one frequency. Most contracts need only one; you add more when different lifts need a different checklist or a different frequency.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick the record template">
    Choose the maintenance checklist this group of lifts is inspected against. Nothing picks it for you, and the builder will not save a group without one. There is nothing for LiftAuth to guess from: a job carries no type of its own — the record template is what makes a visit maintenance, a repair or a breakdown — so there is no job type to resolve a template from, no default template, and nothing derived from the lift.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose Monthly or Quarterly">
    This is how often the lifts in this group are serviced.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Tick the lifts this group covers">
    The building's lifts are listed underneath. A lift can belong to only one coverage group on the contract.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add another group if you need one">
    Click **+ Add record** and repeat. Any lift you leave out is flagged, which is fine if it is serviced under a different contract — the flag is a reminder and does not stop you saving. What does stop you is a group with no record template, or a group with no lifts.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Each generated job inherits its group's record template as a **snapshot taken when the job is generated**. Editing the contract later — swapping a group's template, for instance — does not re-point jobs that already exist; they keep the template they were stamped with. Only visits generated after the edit pick the new one up.

  The template itself is never chosen automatically, but individual form **items** can be: compliance capture items are added to scheduled jobs on their own.
</Note>

<Warning>
  Some templates are written for a single lift and some for a whole set. If you assign several lifts to a single-lift template, the step will tell you and you cannot continue until you split them.
</Warning>

## Step 2 — Recurring billing

This step decides **how the building is invoiced, and for how long the contract runs**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose a billing cadence">
    The [billing cadence](/admin/maintenance-contracts/reference#billing-cadences) is how often LiftAuth drafts an invoice for this contract:

    * **Per-job** — one draft invoice per visit, raised when you close the job.
    * **Annual** — one draft invoice per year.
    * **None** — no automatic invoicing at all; you raise invoices by hand from Billing.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the contract term">
    **Contract start** can be any month, past or future. **Contract end** is the last service month, and must be after the start.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Build the invoice">
    Unless you chose **None**, fill in the invoice lines every generated invoice will carry — the description, quantity and price — plus tax, payment terms and any notes. You can load one of your saved invoice templates from the picker instead of typing it out.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  If you backdate the start, LiftAuth warns you. It schedules from the current month forward — past months will not generate jobs. To log work that already happened, see [How to reschedule and backfill visits](/admin/maintenance-contracts/manage-visits).
</Warning>

<Note>
  The billing cadence and the contract start cannot be changed later. Everything else on this step can.
</Note>

## Step 3 — Annual inspection

This step is about the [**operating certificate**](/start/glossary) each lift renews yearly after its statutory inspection. It is switched off by default — leave it off if this contract does not cover renewals.

Turn on **Cover annual inspection & certificate renewal** and you get:

- The list of covered lifts and their certificate expiry dates. Any lift missing a date is flagged — LiftAuth cannot schedule a renewal for it until you fill one in, and you can type it in right here.
- **Start renewal** — how many months before expiry the renewal should be raised (1 to 12; 3 by default).
- The **renewal quotation** — the total, tax, payment terms and notes for the quote LiftAuth will draft.

When it is on, LiftAuth drafts **one grouped renewal quote for the building** as the earliest covered lift's certificate comes into that window. The quote is a draft only: someone still has to review and send it.

<Note>
  Renewal quotes bill the building manager. A contract with no building manager cannot raise them, and the contract page says so.
</Note>

## Step 4 — Schedule

This step decides **who does the work and when the visits land**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set Assigned to">
    Pick one technician, or one team. Whoever you pick is put on every job this contract generates.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the day of the month">
    The preferred service day. Weekends and public holidays shift to the nearest workday automatically.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set the preferred start time and the estimated visit duration">
    Times are Singapore time. A visit longer than a working day is split across the following workdays.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check the preview calendar">
    The calendar shows every visit the contract will produce. If any of them clash with work already booked, the step tells you — click **Reschedule** on a clashing visit to move just that one, without disturbing the rest.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Step 5 — Review

The last step lays out everything you have chosen — coverage, billing, term, schedule — for a final read. Check it, then create the contract.

## What happens next

- The contract appears in **Maintenance Contracts** with the status **Active** and a reference like `BP-2026-4F91C2`. That reference is shown as **Contract No.**, listed in the contracts table, and searchable, so it is worth noting the form: `BP-`, the year, then six characters. The `BP` is left over from the retired term "Building Plan" — it is not an abbreviation of "Maintenance Contract".
- Its **Upcoming Visits** tab lists every visit for the whole term. The calendar shows the same rows as **planned jobs**, which cannot be dragged.
- Fourteen days before each visit, LiftAuth creates the [job](/admin/jobs/overview), assigns it, and books the time slot. Creating a job notifies nobody on its own; scheduling it notifies the building manager's whole firm — every active user of that firm, not one named contact — by push and email, and pushes to the assigned technician or team.
- Depending on the cadence you chose, draft [invoices](/admin/billing/overview) appear for you to review and send. A contract billed **Per-job** drafts one when you close each job; an **Annual** contract is invoiced on its own yearly schedule instead; **None** never drafts anything.
