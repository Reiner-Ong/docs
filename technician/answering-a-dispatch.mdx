When the call centre raises an emergency call-out, your phone rings with a siren — even if the app is closed. This page is what to do next.

<Note>
  A dispatch is not a [job](/start/glossary). No job has been scheduled for you. If the call-out turns into work that needs a record, create the job on site — see [Create an adhoc job](/technician/create-an-adhoc-job).
</Note>

## The ring

Tap the notification and the call-out opens as a sheet with:

* a red badge saying **Entrapment** (with the number of people inside) or **Lift fault**,
* the building you are being asked to go to,
* what the caller reported,
* who else is being rung, and whether they have answered.

If you swipe the sheet away without deciding, a red bar keeps blinking above the tabs at the bottom of the app: *Incoming dispatch — tap to answer*. Dismissing the sheet never stops the ring. If more than one emergency is live, the bar says how many and tapping it lets you pick.

## Accept or decline

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the badge first">
    **Entrapment** means people are stuck inside a lift right now.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Tap Accept dispatch, or Decline">
    Whoever accepts first takes the call-out. Everyone else's phone stops ringing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If someone beat you to it, the sheet tells you so — **"[Name] is on it"** — and there is nothing more for you to do. If you decline, the call centre sees it immediately and can ring someone else.

## Once you have accepted

The sheet becomes **You're responding**, and the call centre and the other technicians all know you are on the way. You now have:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Call">
    Dials the person who reported the problem, if the operator captured a number. Worth doing on an entrapment — you can tell them help is coming.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Navigate to building">
    Starts sharing your live location toward the building. The call centre then sees where you are, how far out you are and roughly when you will arrive, so they can answer the caller without ringing you. Sharing keeps running in the background while you drive, and stops on its own when you arrive or release the call-out.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I've arrived">
    Tap this when you are on site. It stamps your arrival time and stops the live share.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can't make it — release this dispatch">
    Hands the call-out back. It starts ringing the other technicians again from scratch, so use it as soon as you know, not when you are already late.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
  If **Live sharing off — no location permission** appears, your phone is blocking location for LiftAuth. You can still attend the call-out; the call centre just cannot see you moving. Fix it in your phone's settings for the LiftAuth app.
</Warning>

If the call centre stands the call-out down while you are on the way, the sheet tells you: **Callout stood down — nothing more to do.**

## Being on call

Your firm keeps an on-call rota. When you are on it, a blue **On call** bar sits above the tabs, with the time your standby ends.

You can also switch yourself on or off manually:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Profile">
    Tap **Profile** in the bottom tabs.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Find the ON CALL section">
    Tap the **On call** row to flip the switch.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check the schedule">
    Tap **On-call schedule** to see the rota — yours and everyone else's. It is read-only; only an owner or admin can change shifts.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Whichever happened most recently wins. Switching yourself off during a rostered shift takes you off the top of the operator's list — but it does not stop anyone ringing you, because an operator can ring any technician in an emergency.
