Use this page as a reference whenever you come across an unfamiliar word in LiftAuth.

## The basics

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Lift">
    The physical elevator unit at a building. Each lift has its own entry in LiftAuth with details like location, type, manufacturer, and serial number.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Building">
    A site with one or more lifts in it. Buildings organise most of LiftAuth: a job visits one building, a maintenance contract covers one building, and Drive files records under the building they belong to. A building has one **Building Manager** — the company responsible for it, which is not the same thing as the building itself.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Building Manager">
    The company or owner responsible for a building. They receive the paperwork for work done on their lifts, sign off completed visits, and are billed for them. Some screens call them the **customer** — it is the same thing.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Tenant">
    The company that uses LiftAuth to manage and maintain lifts. Everything in LiftAuth — lifts, jobs, invoices, team members — belongs to one Tenant. If you work for one company, you will only ever see one Tenant. Some screens say **workspace** instead; it is the same thing.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Team">
    Careful — this word is used two ways. In **Manage Your Team** it means everyone at your company who has a LiftAuth login. On a job it means a **crew**: a named group of technicians who attend a visit together and can each submit part of the work.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="OTP (One-Time Password)">
    A 6-digit code sent to your phone by SMS or to your email. You use it to log in instead of a traditional password. It expires after a few minutes.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Who does what

Permission tables throughout these guides use these role names. You can see your own under **Manage Your Team**.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Owner">
    Runs the workspace. Can do everything, including billing settings, features, and adding or removing people.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Admin">
    Office staff. Runs the day-to-day work — buildings, contracts, jobs, records and billing — but not workspace-level settings.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Technician">
    Field staff. Works mainly in the mobile app: picks up assigned jobs, fills in records, gets them signed. Filling in a record is a mobile action — there is no screen for it in the web dashboard. A technician can also sign in to the web dashboard, where they see most areas but not Billing, the Inbox, Record Templates or Projects.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Call Center">
    Takes emergency calls and raises dispatches. The sidebar shows them the dispatch console and little else. Spelled "Call Center" on screen, which is the spelling these guides use in permission tables.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Work on site

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Job">
    One visit's worth of work at one building. A job holds:

    - the **building** being visited,
    - one or more **lifts** to be worked on,
    - one or more **forms** for the technician to fill in,
    - exactly one **assignee** — a technician, or a team of them.

    A job does not itself have a type. Whether the visit is maintenance, a breakdown call-out or a repair comes from the record template attached to it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Form">
    The everyday word for a record template once it is attached to a job — the thing a technician actually fills in. A job holds one or more. Fill one in and submit it and it becomes a **record**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Coverage group">
    One record template paired with the lifts it covers. A maintenance contract is built out of these: each group says which lifts get which form, and how often. The contract builder labels them "Record 1", "Record 2" on screen — they are **not** the signed reports a technician submits.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Record Template">
    The form design that decides what a technician is asked on site — the items to check, the readings to take, the photos to attach. Your company sets these up once and reuses them. You may also use an official form from the built-in library.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Item">
    One line on a record template that the technician answers — typically Pass or Fail, but it can also be a number, a note, or a photo.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Record">
    The report produced when a technician fills in a form and submits it. It holds the item results, photos, notes, and signatures, and can be downloaded as a PDF.

    A record cannot be corrected. Once it exists, nobody can edit it — there is no amend and no resubmit. A signed record cannot even be deleted; an unsigned one can be deleted by an owner or admin, but not changed.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Signature">
    The sign-off confirming work was completed. Normally both the technician and the building manager sign. A technician who cannot get a signature on site can **skip signing**, which finishes the job without a building-manager signature on the PDF.

    Skipping is final: the record can never be sent for signature afterwards, and any signing link already issued stops working. Note that the dashboard shows a skipped record as **Signed**, the same as one the building manager really signed — only the PDF shows the difference, by leaving the building-manager signature out.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Signing link">
    A one-time web link that lets a building manager sign a record on their own device. A technician can generate and share one, or an owner or admin can email it from the record. Each link works once and expires after 7 days, and generating a new one does not cancel earlier ones. Whoever opens the link can sign without a LiftAuth account.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Issue / Fault">
    A problem found on a lift. Issues are given a severity level — Critical, Major, or Minor — and tracked until they are resolved.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Dispatch">
    How LiftAuth finds a technician for a **call-out**. A dispatcher picks which phones to ring; they all ring at once, and the first to accept owns it. A dispatch creates no job, record or invoice of its own.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Call-out">
    One emergency attendance: the thing that happens when a lift has stopped working or someone is trapped. A dispatcher raises a **dispatch** to find a technician for it. A call-out is not a job — if it turns into recorded work, that is a separate breakdown job.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="On call">
    Marked as available for emergency call-outs outside normal hours. The on-call rota ranks the technician list so the dispatcher can see who is on standby — it does not ring anyone by itself, and it never limits who can be rung. An operator always picks the phones.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Entrapment">
    Someone is trapped inside a lift. The most urgent kind of dispatch.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Stand down">
    Calling off a dispatch that is no longer needed — the lift freed itself, or somebody else has dealt with it. The technicians' phones stop ringing.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Event">
    Anything other than a job that takes up someone's time on the calendar — leave, training, a meeting. Events can repeat.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Planned job">
    A visit a maintenance contract is going to create but has not created yet. It shows on the calendar so you can see what is coming, but it is not a real job you can open until 14 days before the visit.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Operating certificate">
    The permit a lift needs to stay in service in Singapore. It is renewed yearly after a statutory inspection, and a maintenance contract can track when it is due.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="BCA Equipment ID">
    A unique code assigned to a lift under Singapore building compliance regulations. LiftAuth can look up lift details automatically using this code.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Scheduling and agreements

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Maintenance Contract">
    Your servicing agreement with a building: which lifts are covered, how often they are visited, which forms are used, and how the work is billed. Once set up, LiftAuth creates the visits for you. Whether it also drafts the invoices depends on the contract's billing cadence — per job, annually, or not at all.

    Contracts are set per **building**, not per lift. Older guidance called this a "maintenance plan".
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Project">
    A larger piece of work that runs over several visits and documents — a modernisation, an installation, a major repair — tracked through stages from quoting to completion. Use a project when one job is not enough to hold the work.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Stage">
    One step of a project. Every project has the same six, in order: Supplier quote, Customer quote, Procurement, Work, Invoice, Closed. They cannot be added to, renamed or reordered. A stage can be completed or skipped, and the project moves on when its stages do.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Money

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Quote">
    A price you send a building manager before doing the work. If they accept it, you can turn it into an invoice.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Invoice">
    The bill sent to a building manager for work done. Invoices can come from a maintenance contract automatically, from a project, or be written by hand.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Draft invoice">
    An invoice that exists but has not been issued yet. You can still change it. Nothing reaches the customer until you **issue** it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Issuing">
    Finalising a quote or invoice so it counts as a real document — it gets its reference number and can no longer be freely edited. Issuing is not the same as emailing it; those are two separate steps.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Payment">
    Money received against an invoice, recorded so you can see what is still outstanding.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Credit note">
    A document that cancels or reduces an invoice you have already issued — for an over-charge, a refund, or a write-off.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Statement of account">
    A summary of everything one customer owes you and how overdue each amount is.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Ageing">
    How long an unpaid amount has been outstanding, grouped into four bands: **Current**, **31–60 days**, **61–90 days** and **90+ days**. Careful — Current means not yet due _or_ up to 30 days overdue, so an invoice a fortnight late sits in Current with no badge, even though the ledger marks it Overdue.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Standard billing item">
    A saved line you bill often — a callout fee, an hourly rate — so you do not retype it on every document.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Reference number">
    The identifier printed on a quote, invoice or credit note. You can set the format your firm uses.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Ledger">
    The single list in Billing holding issued quotes, invoices, payments and credit notes together. It is a page, not a complete history: it loads at most 500 of each kind, and the totals at the top describe only what is on the page. Filter to one customer to narrow it, and use a **Statement of account** when you need everything that customer owes. Drafts are not on the ledger.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Deposit (billing)">
    An up-front part-payment taken before the work is done. Not to be confused with a stock **deposit**, which means putting parts into a warehouse.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Supplier">
    A company you buy parts or subcontracted work from. Projects track what your supplier quotes you separately from what you quote the customer.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Purchase order">
    The order you place with a supplier. On a project this is its own stage, and it is internal — the building manager never sees it.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Documents and stock

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Drive">
    Where LiftAuth keeps your documents. Records land here automatically when a technician submits them, filed under the building they belong to. Past visits you type in by hand land here too. There is also a folder per customer holding their quotes, invoices and credit notes — a payment has no document of its own.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="PDF">
    A downloadable document version of a completed record, including all item results, photos, and signatures.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Part">
    A component you fit or replace — a door roller, a contactor. Parts live in a catalogue with a part number and a price.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Warehouse">
    A place you keep stock: a store, a workshop, or a van.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Stock on hand">
    How many of a part you currently hold, counted per warehouse. LiftAuth does not deduct stock by itself when a part is used on a job — withdrawals are recorded by hand.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Stock movement">
    Any change to stock, recorded so the count stays honest. There are three kinds:

    - **Deposit** — stock arriving into a warehouse.
    - **Withdraw** — stock leaving it, typically to be fitted on a job.
    - **Transfer** — stock moving from one warehouse to another, such as store to van.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Part No. and part code">
    Two different numbers. **Part No.** is LiftAuth's own reference, like `PRT-2026-A1B2C3`. It is assigned for you, cannot be edited, and — along with the part's name — is what the search box matches. The **part code** is the manufacturer's code stamped on the physical part. You type it in or let a photo read it, you can edit it, and the search box does **not** match it.

    There is no field for your firm's own existing internal part numbers.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Library form">
    An official form — for example a BCA inspection form — that LiftAuth supplies ready-made, so you do not have to build the record template yourself.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Unassigned">
    The folder in Drive holding records for lifts that are not attached to any building. It is listed after the real building folders.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
